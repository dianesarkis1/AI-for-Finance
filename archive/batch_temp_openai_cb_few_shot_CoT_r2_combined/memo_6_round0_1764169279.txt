# Investment Memorandum

## 1. Executive Summary

- On July 25, 2025, Universal Electronics Inc. (a Delaware corporation) entered into a tenth amendment to their second amended and restated credit agreement with U.S. Bank National Association as Administrative Agent and Lender.
- The original credit agreement dates back to October 27, 2017, and has been amended nine times previously, indicating an active and evolving credit relationship.
- The primary purpose of this amendment is to modify the permitted liens covenant, specifically increasing the allowable indebtedness secured by liens in favor of Bank of China from an unspecified previous amount to $21,000,000, with the restriction that such liens cannot extend to any U.S. property.
- Universal Electronics Inc. operates as a technology company, with Universal Electronics BV serving as a guarantor under a continuing guaranty dated October 25, 2021. The credit facility is secured by various security documents including security agreements, trademark grants, patent grants, and guaranties.

## 2. Investment Highlights & Risks

### Key Strengths / Credit Positives:
- Established banking relationship with U.S. Bank National Association, a major national banking institution
- Long-term credit relationship spanning multiple years with regular amendments suggesting ongoing lender engagement and borrower compliance
- Strong security package including intellectual property (patents and trademarks) and general security agreements
- International guarantor support from Universal Electronics BV
- Geographic restriction on foreign liens protects U.S.-based collateral

### Principal Risks and Mitigating Factors:
- Increased permitted foreign indebtedness to $21,000,000 may indicate growing leverage or financing needs outside the U.S.
- Multiple amendments (now tenth amendment) could suggest ongoing covenant management challenges or changing business needs
- Limited visibility into overall financial performance, total facility size, or other key credit metrics
- Release provisions in favor of lenders may limit borrower's future claims against lenders
