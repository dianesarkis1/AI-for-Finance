# Investment Memorandum

## 1. Executive Summary

**Date of Agreement:** 2025-07-25

**Borrower/Company Overview:** Universal Electronics Inc. is a Delaware corporation with operations supported by its subsidiary Universal Electronics BV, which serves as guarantor under the credit facility. The company maintains intellectual property assets including trademarks and patents that secure the credit arrangement.

**Transaction Description:** This transaction represents the tenth amendment to Universal Electronics Inc.'s existing credit facility with U.S. Bank National Association, which serves as both Lender and Administrative Agent. The original Second Amended and Restated Credit Agreement was established on October 27, 2017, with nine subsequent amendments demonstrating an active, long-term banking relationship.

**Purpose of Financing:** The amendment specifically modifies the Liens covenant to accommodate increased secured indebtedness with Bank of China, raising the permitted threshold to $21,000,000 while maintaining geographic restrictions that protect U.S.-based assets.

**Company Background:** Universal Electronics operates under a comprehensive security structure including Security Agreements dating to 2012, intellectual property grants covering trademarks and patents, and subsidiary guarantees. The borrower has confirmed compliance with all existing covenant requirements and absence of defaults.

## 2. Investment Highlights & Risks

**Key Strengths / Credit Positives:**
• Established eight-year credit relationship with U.S. Bank National Association demonstrates institutional confidence and operational stability
• Comprehensive collateral package including Security Agreements, intellectual property rights, and subsidiary guaranty from Universal Electronics BV
• Geographic covenant restrictions prevent Chinese bank liens from extending to U.S. property, protecting domestic asset base
• Current compliance status with no reported defaults across Credit Agreement, Note, or Security Documents
• Active covenant management through structured amendment process indicates proactive borrower engagement

**Principal Risks and Mitigating Factors:**
• Increased foreign secured debt capacity ($21,000,000 with Bank of China) introduces potential subordination and cross-default risks, though geographic limitations provide partial mitigation
• Frequent amendment activity (ten modifications since 2017) may signal ongoing financial covenant pressure or evolving capital needs
• Limited transparency into overall facility size, current utilization levels, and borrower financial metrics restricts comprehensive credit assessment
• Cross-border exposure through Bank of China relationship introduces regulatory and operational complexity
