# Investment Memorandum

## 1. Executive Summary

**Date of Agreement:** 2025-07-25

**Borrower/Company Overview:** Universal Electronics Inc., a Delaware corporation, is the borrower under a comprehensive credit facility with U.S. Bank National Association.

**Transaction Description:** This transaction represents the tenth amendment to Universal Electronics Inc.'s Second Amended and Restated Credit Agreement originally executed October 27, 2017. U.S. Bank National Association serves as Administrative Agent, Lender, and LC Issuer under the facility.

**Purpose of Financing:** The amendment modifies existing lien provisions to permit liens in favor of Bank of China securing up to $21,000,000 in indebtedness, restricted to non-U.S. property only.

**Company Background:** Universal Electronics Inc. maintains an established credit relationship spanning multiple years with comprehensive security arrangements. The borrower operates with subsidiary Universal Electronics BV providing guaranty support and maintains intellectual property assets including trademarks and patents as part of the collateral structure.

## 2. Investment Highlights & Risks

**Key Strengths / Credit Positives:**
• Established, long-term banking relationship with U.S. Bank National Association in multiple capacities (Administrative Agent, Lender, LC Issuer)
• Comprehensive security package including security agreements, intellectual property collateral (trademarks and patents), and subsidiary guaranty from Universal Electronics BV
• Borrower confirms current compliance with all credit agreement terms and absence of defaults
• Geographic limitation on new permitted liens restricts Bank of China security interests to non-U.S. assets only
• Strong legal framework with detailed documentation and continuing guaranty support

**Principal Risks and Mitigating Factors:**
• Extensive amendment history (ten amendments since 2017) may indicate ongoing covenant management challenges or evolving business requirements
• Introduction of additional secured creditor (Bank of China) creates potential for inter-creditor complexity, though mitigated by geographic restrictions and dollar limitations
• Limited visibility into current financial performance, borrowing capacity utilization, and overall credit metrics
• Frequent modifications to credit terms may signal underlying business volatility or changing capital needs
