# Investment Memorandum

## 1. Executive Summary

- On October 29, 2024, MarineMax, Inc., a Florida-based boat and yacht dealer, executed the Fourth Amendment to its Credit Agreement originally dated August 8, 2022.
- The transaction involves MarineMax as Borrower Representative along with numerous subsidiary guarantors including boat dealerships, marinas, yacht services companies, and related entities across multiple jurisdictions.
- The credit facility is administered by Manufacturers and Traders Trust Company (M&T Bank) as Administrative Agent, with Wells Fargo Commercial Distribution Finance serving as Floor Plan Agent. The lender syndicate includes major financial institutions: Bank of America, PNC Bank, BMO Bank, The Huntington National Bank, BankUnited, Raymond James Bank, and several regional lenders.
- The primary purpose of this amendment is to modify Section 2.01(a)(iii) of the existing credit agreement, which governs floor plan financing limits for different categories of boats and yachts. The amendment establishes specific sublimits within the floor plan facility: $158,333,333 each for boats larger than 80 feet, foreign OEM units (excluding Azimut and Galeon brands), and used floor plan units, plus a $450,000,000 limit for individual units valued over $1,000,000.

## 2. Investment Highlights & Risks

### Key Strengths / Credit Positives:
- Strong lender consortium featuring established financial institutions with marine lending expertise, indicating continued confidence in MarineMax's credit profile
- Comprehensive guarantee structure with numerous subsidiary entities providing credit enhancement across MarineMax's diversified operations
- The amendment represents an operational enhancement rather than distressed restructuring, suggesting ongoing business optimization
- Reaffirmation of all existing collateral documents and security interests maintains strong creditor protections

### Principal Risks and Mitigating Factors:
- The amendment provides more borrower-friendly sublimit structures for floor plan financing, potentially increasing credit exposure in specific boat categories
- Marine retail industry exposure to economic cycles and discretionary consumer spending patterns
- Limited visibility into overall facility size, pricing, and key financial maintenance covenants constrains comprehensive credit assessment
- Complex multi-jurisdictional subsidiary structure may present operational and legal complexity
