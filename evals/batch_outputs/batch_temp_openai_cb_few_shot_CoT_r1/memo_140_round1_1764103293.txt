# Investment Memorandum

## 1. Executive Summary

**Date of Agreement:** 2021-01-28

**Borrower / Company Overview:** Square, Inc. serves as the borrower under this revolving credit facility, supported by a syndicate of major financial institutions.

**Transaction Description:** This transaction represents a third amendment to Square Inc.'s revolving credit agreement originally dated May 1, 2020. The credit facility is structured with Goldman Sachs Bank USA as Administrative Agent and includes a consortium of lenders: JPMorgan Chase Bank, Wells Fargo Bank, Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation, HSBC Bank USA, and U.S. Bank National Association.

**Purpose of Financing:** The amendment specifically accommodates Square's participation in COVID-19 relief programs by updating regulatory definitions and increasing permitted borrowing capacity under the Paycheck Protection Program Liquidity Facility (PPPLF) to $1,000,000,000 aggregate principal amount outstanding.

**Company Background:** Square Inc. operates as a financial services and digital payments company. The borrower maintains an established banking relationship with top-tier financial institutions and demonstrates active participation in government-backed liquidity programs during the COVID-19 pandemic period.

## 2. Investment Highlights & Risks

**Key Strengths / Credit Positives:**
• Strong banking consortium comprising top-tier financial institutions (Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan Chase, Wells Fargo, HSBC, U.S. Bank, Sumitomo Mitsui), demonstrating significant lender confidence in Square's credit profile
• Borrower maintains compliance with all existing covenants and representations, with no defaults or events of default occurring
• Strategic participation in government-backed COVID relief programs provides additional liquidity support and demonstrates proactive capital management
• Amendment executed smoothly with all required lender approvals obtained

**Principal Risks and Mitigating Factors:**
• The substantial increase in PPPLF borrowing capacity to $1 billion may indicate potential liquidity needs or market stress related to COVID-19 impacts
• Limited visibility into core financial metrics, pricing terms, and other covenant details constrains comprehensive credit assessment
• Regulatory dependency on government programs creates potential exposure to policy changes or program modifications
• Amendment nature limits insight into overall facility structure and borrower financial performance
