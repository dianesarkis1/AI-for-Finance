# Investment Memorandum

## 1. Executive Summary

- On October 12, 2023, EverBank N.A. joined as an additional lender to an existing revolving credit and security agreement for MSCC Funding I, LLC.
- MSCC Funding I, LLC, a Delaware limited liability company, serves as the borrower, with Main Street Capital Corporation acting as collateral manager. The original credit agreement was dated November 22, 2022.
- The transaction involves EverBank joining a syndicated lending group that includes Truist Bank as administrative agent and swingline lender, Citibank N.A. as collateral agent, custodian and document custodian, and Virtus Group LP as collateral administrator.
- The purpose is to add EverBank as a new lender with a $75,000,000 commitment to the existing revolving credit facility, expanding the total lending capacity of the syndicate.
- Main Street Capital Corporation, a Maryland corporation business development company, appears to be utilizing this funding structure through its subsidiary MSCC Funding I, LLC for investment activities requiring qualified purchaser and qualified institutional buyer status.

## 2. Investment Highlights & Risks

### Key Strengths / Credit Positives:
- Strong institutional lending syndicate including major banks (Truist Bank, Citibank N.A., and now EverBank N.A.), indicating broad lender confidence
- Structured as a revolving credit facility providing operational flexibility for the borrower
- Comprehensive security and collateral framework with dedicated collateral agent, custodian, and administrator roles
- Qualified institutional buyer and qualified purchaser requirements suggest sophisticated investment-grade structure
- Main Street Capital Corporation backing provides established BDC sponsor support

### Principal Risks and Mitigating Factors:
- Limited visibility into pricing, covenants, or specific collateral details due to redacted information
- Revolving credit structure creates potential for variable utilization and cash flow timing
- Investment company structure may be subject to regulatory constraints and market volatility
